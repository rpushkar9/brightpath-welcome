# Demo script

The timings below are estimates, because nobody has rehearsed this aloud yet. The target is about 4 minutes 40 seconds, and the assignment allows 2 to 5 minutes.

## Before you record

1. Record on a weekday. On a weekend, Emily no longer appears on the first day.
2. Stop the tours opening by themselves, so they do not pop up on camera. When the tour card appears, clear **Open this tour on every reload**, then click **Skip tour**. This applies to every person in that browser.
3. Open the **View as** menu and choose **Reset demo data**, so dates and progress are fresh. This does not change the tour setting.
4. Open two browser windows side by side:
   - Left: `#/me/emily-carter`
   - Right: `#/team/david`
5. Rehearse once, then reset again.

## 0:00 What this is and who it is for (25 seconds)

Show Emily's home screen.

> BrightPath has about 150 employees, and onboarding lives across emails, documents, spreadsheets, and messages from HR. New hires miss steps, and managers can't tell what got done.
>
> I built one place for this. Each person gets a clear answer to their own question: what do I do next, who needs my help, and where does our process keep breaking.

## 0:25 How I handled the disagreement (25 seconds)

Point at the three tasks, then at the Engineering label.

> David, an engineering manager, doesn't want everyone on the same checklist. Emily, a new hire, doesn't want to see 40 things on day one. I don't think they disagree. David is talking about which tasks exist, and Emily is talking about how many appear at once.
>
> So Emily's plan has 18 tasks, 7 of them specific to Engineering. This screen shows three, and it says where to start.

## 0:50 The new hire (65 seconds)

Click **Mark complete**. Point at the counts and the next task. Point at **Waiting on someone else**, click **Ask for help**, then **Send request**.

> When a task is finished, progress updates right away and the next task moves up, with Undo for mistakes. Checking it off is only the person's own confirmation, and the screen says it doesn't submit anything.
>
> Emily can't run the local setup until IT grants access. The app says so, and it doesn't count that against Emily. One click sends a prepared request to the person who can act.

Type "Who can help me with repository access?" and press Enter.

> People have a lot of questions at the start. These answers come from Emily's real plan using simple rules, and the panel says it isn't AI.

## 1:55 Department differences (10 seconds)

Use **View as** to open Marcus Webb.

> Marcus is in Sales, so the department tasks are different, and overdue work comes first, worded calmly.

## 2:05 The manager (30 seconds)

Put the left window back on Emily. Complete a task on the left, and point at the right.

> David's page answers one question: who needs my help. When Emily finishes a task, it shows up here, because the app keeps a real activity log rather than guessing.
>
> Priya's card says why, and what to do next. David can see which work is David's to do, and which is with IT, so nobody chases the wrong person.

Click **Nudge Priya**.

## 2:35 HR (30 seconds)

Switch the right window to **Sarah Mitchell**. In **Help requests**, click **Mark resolved** on Emily's request. On Tomás's card, click **Open task**, then **Mark complete**, and close the drawer.

> Sarah sees every team. Emily's request is already here, and resolving it updates Emily's screen. Tomás is flagged because an IT task is late, so nudging Tomás wouldn't help, and the app doesn't offer that. Sarah clears it here.

## 3:05 Where people get stuck (40 seconds)

Click **Insights**. Open **Create CRM account**, and click **Propose a plan improvement**.

> This is what I'd most like Sarah to see. Fixing one person's problem doesn't fix the process. This shows which tasks trip up more than one hire. Here, CRM access affects all three Sales hires.
>
> I was careful about what this can claim. It's a small sample and the screen says so. It counts each person once, and it says what the records can't show, like why something ran late. The suggestion is labelled as a suggestion. Sarah can record a proposal, and it doesn't change anyone's plan or any legal requirement.

## 3:45 What I guessed (15 seconds)

> I wrote down my assumptions. Two that matter: upcoming tasks can't be completed early, and "needs attention" means two overdue tasks, or one that's three business days late.

## 4:00 What I left out, and what I'd ask Sarah (25 seconds)

> I left out real login, documents, e-signature, email, a template editor, and a real AI model. A model would need a secure backend. It should answer only from sources it can cite, send sensitive questions to HR, and never mark a task done or judge an employee.
>
> Next, I'd ask Sarah which tasks every department must do, which dates are legally fixed, and who chases IT today.

## 4:25 How I checked the AI's work, and time (15 seconds)

Show `docs/ai-review-log.md` briefly.

> I built this with Claude Code, with independent AI reviews, and kept a log of what they caught. One example: every test passed while a spotlight pointed at empty space, and I only saw it by looking. The first version took about three hours fifteen, a little past the limit. I then chose to keep improving it, and the history shows that.

## If something goes wrong while recording

- To restart, use **View as**, then **Reset demo data**.
- If a date looks off, reset. Dates are set when the sample data is created.
